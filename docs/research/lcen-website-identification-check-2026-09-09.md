# LCEN website identification check

Research date: 9 September 2026. This note is source research for a possible
scanner/dashboard issue. It is not a legal opinion and does not conclude that
any named website or operator has breached an obligation.

## Current statutory basis

The operative publisher-identification provision is article 1-1 of the law
for confidence in the digital economy (LCEN), in force since 23 May 2024. It
was moved there by law no. 2024-449 of 21 May 2024. The former reference to
LCEN article 6 III is superseded and must not be used for a current finding.

Under [LCEN, article 1-1 I](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/article_lc/LEGIARTI000049568614), a person whose activity is editing an online public
communication service must make the following information available to the
public in an open standard:

- A natural person: name, given names, domicile and telephone number. If the
  person is subject to registration with the RCS or, for a craft business, the
  RNE, the registration number is also required.
- A legal person: company or business name, registered office and telephone
  number. If it is subject to those registration formalities, the registration
  number, share capital and registered-office address are also required.
- The publication director or co-director and, where applicable, the editorial
  manager.
- The host's name or company name, address and telephone number.
- Where applicable, the identity and address of a party that stores data it
  processes directly when editing the service.

The anonymous-editor exception is limited to a person editing
non-professionally. It permits publication of only the host's name/company
name and address, provided the editor has supplied the host with the personal
identification information specified by the statute. The statute calls for the
information to be made available. It does not prescribe a route or a page
heading named `Mentions légales`.

Article 1-2 contains the statutory penalty for non-compliance with article
1-1 I or II. It is not a client-specific sanction assessment and must not be
presented as one. See [LCEN, article 1-2](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000049568616/).

## Registered business information

[Code de commerce, article R. 123-237](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000045710304) additionally states that every registered person
must show on its website its unique company identification number, the words
`RCS` followed by the city of the registry, and the particulars listed in that
article, including its registered office. The statutory rule has conditions
for foreign commercial companies and certain individual-business forms.

The code describes the identifier allocated to a legal unit as a nine-digit
number, and an establishment identifier as that nine-digit number followed by
five establishment-specific digits. See [Code de commerce, article
R. 123-221](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000005634379/LEGISCTA000006146213/).
The [Insee's official Sirene documentation](https://www.insee.fr/fr/information/6043016)
names these identifiers SIREN for the enterprise or legal unit and SIRET for
the establishment. The statutory website rules above require a registration
number or unique company identifier in the stated conditions. They do not make
a SIRET, as distinct from a SIREN, the universal required field.

## Safe audit observation and detection boundary

The defensible observation is limited to what the scan can establish, for
example: “No publisher-identification information or recognised registration
identifier was found on the first-party pages visited.” A missing page whose
link text says `Mentions légales` is not enough on its own, because the law
requires availability of information rather than a specific page label.

The scanner should retain the pages, links and extracted evidence it checked,
and disclose its coverage. Legal information may be on a page that was not
visited, an external booking route, or an inaccessible document. It should not
say that an operator has no legal notices, is acting illegally, is in breach,
or faces a specified sanction. It should state the legal criterion, the
information not found in the scanned material, and recommend confirmation
with a qualified legal adviser.
