# Odysseus Business-Impact Statistics Reference Database: Accessibility, SEO & GEO
## Vetted, Sourced Statistics for Client-Facing Audit Reports (French B2C SMBs — Hospitality & Consumer Sectors)

## TL;DR
- **Accessibility and SEO both have citable, methodologically-disclosed business-impact statistics you can put in front of prospects today; GEO (AI-answer visibility) does not yet have citable *revenue*-impact data — treat all GEO revenue/conversion claims as emerging/preliminary.** The strongest "safe to cite" anchors are the Deloitte/Google *Milliseconds Make Millions* speed-conversion study (2020), Baymard Institute's checkout research (2025), the Pew Research Center AI Overviews study (July 2025), and the W3C-documented Legal & General case study.
- **The "Web Standards Commission" (wsc.us.org) is NOT a credible source and must be excluded.** It is a self-described "independent private advisory organization" that sells/recommends compliance tools, presents itself with fabricated authority ("America's Digital Accessibility Authority," "initiating compliance proceedings"), and publishes a $13B→$18B economic-impact figure with no verifiable methodology. Its numbers circulate only within its own site and SEO-bait roundups. Do not confuse it with the legitimate **W3C/WAI** (w3.org/WAI).
- **For French SMBs specifically:** the EAA has applied since 28 June 2025; micro-enterprises (<10 employees AND <€2M turnover/balance sheet) are exempt from service requirements, so many of Odysseus's targets are legally in-scope. The correct French penalty for private e-commerce is **7,500€ per infraction (cumulative), 15,000€ on repeat** (Code de la consommation, art. R451-4), enforced by the DGCCRF — not the widely-miscited "up to 250,000€."

## SOURCE-QUALITY VERDICT ON "WEB STANDARDS COMMISSION" (wsc.us.org)

**Verdict: EXCLUDE. Low-credibility / do not cite under any circumstances.**

Findings from direct review of wsc.us.org:
- Its "About" page describes it as *"an independent private advisory organization established in response to the growing number of accessibility lawsuits"* whose mission is *"conducting thorough audits and recommending the right tools."* This is a commercial actor with a sales interest, not a standards body.
- It simultaneously brands itself *"America's Digital Accessibility Authority"* and publishes articles claiming it is *"initiating compliance proceedings,"* will *"establish comprehensive compliance roadmaps,"* and will send *"formal notices within 30 days"* — language impersonating a regulator. It holds no such legal authority.
- Its headline economic figure — *"businesses lose approximately $13 billion annually due to inaccessible websites"* rising to *"$18 billion annually by 2027"* (wsc.us.org/article/9) — claims to analyze "50,000+ websites and 2.8 million user sessions" over "18 months" and to have "received peer review from leading economic research institutions," but names **no** institution, authors, journal, dataset, or published methodology. It is unverifiable.
- Note the deliberate confusability of "WSC" with the real, legitimate body, **W3C / Web Accessibility Initiative (WAI)** (w3.org/WAI). There is no established, independent organization called the "Web Standards Commission."

**Rule for Odysseus:** Any accessibility "statistic" whose only traceable origin is wsc.us.org (or a roundup citing it) must be dropped. Textbook category (c): vague/unverifiable authority, no methodology.

# SECTION 1 — WEB ACCESSIBILITY

## 1.1 General / Cross-Sector Statistics

### SAFE TO CITE BY NAME

**Deloitte & Google — *Milliseconds Make Millions* (2020).** A 0.1-second improvement in mobile site speed was associated with **+8.4% retail conversions and +9.2% average order value**; **+10.1% travel conversions and +1.9% AOV**; luxury page-views-per-session +8.6%; lead-generation bounce rate −8.3%. Based on **~30 million user sessions across 37 brands** (retail figures drawn from 15 brands / 20.5M sessions) in Europe and the US, at a 90% confidence level, isolating speed as the variable. *Credibility: HIGH* — disclosed methodology, large sample, reputable analyst firm + Google. Directly relevant because accessibility remediation (semantic markup, reduced DOM bloat, cleaner forms, faster rendering) improves the same UX/speed levers. **Scales down well** (percentage multipliers applicable to any business's revenue/AOV). *Caveat: correlational, mobile-only, uses pre-Core-Web-Vitals metrics; Deloitte's own 2025 update affirms the findings still hold directionally.*

**Nielsen Norman Group — *Return on Investment (ROI) for Usability*, 4th ed.** Across 72 redesign projects, average post-redesign improvements were **+87% sales/conversions, +91% traffic, +112% user productivity, +174% use of target features.** *Credibility: MEDIUM-HIGH* — NN/g is a top independent UX research firm and the report is a named, purchasable primary source; but the figures are averages of self-selected redesign case studies (selection/survivorship bias) and pre-date 2010. Present as "usability-redesign" evidence, not a precise forecast. **Scales as directional multipliers only.**

**Baymard Institute — cart/checkout research (2025).** The current running average documented cart-abandonment rate is **70.22%** (last updated 22 September 2025), a meta-analysis averaging **50 studies (2006–2025)**; Baymard's own page states: *"we've calculated the average cart abandonment rate of 70.22%… based on 50 different studies"* (baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate). (The earlier and still widely-quoted **70.19% / 49 studies** figure is the July 2023 vintage — either is defensible if dated correctly.) Baymard estimates best-practice checkout design can lift conversions by **up to 35.26%** on the average large e-commerce site. In Baymard's 2024 survey (n=4,329 US adults), the top *fixable* abandonment reasons were **extra costs (shipping, tax, fees) — 39%; mandatory account creation — 19%; a too-long/complicated checkout — 18%** (among shoppers who abandoned for a reason other than browsing). *Credibility: HIGH* — independent research institute, disclosed methodology, the single most-cited checkout benchmark. **Scales well** (percentages). Accessibility relevance: keyboard operability, labeled fields, and clear error messaging are direct fixes to these same friction points.

**W3C Web Accessibility Initiative (WAI) — *The Business Case for Digital Accessibility.*** Authoritative framing of accessibility benefits (innovation, brand, market reach, legal-risk reduction) with a bibliography. Its "over 1 billion people / >$6 trillion spending power" figure is a **population/spending-power stat excluded by the task — do not use it as a business-impact number.** Value for Odysseus: authoritative framing plus documented named case studies. *Credibility: HIGH for framing.*

**Legal & General (documented by W3C WAI, archived case study).** After an accessibility-driven redesign, W3C records precisely: **"Site visitors receiving quotations doubled within 3 months; 100% return on investment (ROI) in 12 months,"** plus **maintenance-cost savings of £200,000/year** and **page-load times reduced by 75%.** L&G's then-Customer Experience Director Caroline Fawcett is quoted: *"The new site has almost doubled the number of visitors seeking quotes and buying Legal & General financial products online. It has cut maintenance costs by two thirds and increased the amount of natural search traffic we get by half as much again"* (≈+50% organic search traffic). *Credibility: MEDIUM-HIGH as a named, W3C-documented case study* — exactly the "specific and checkable" category the task prioritizes. *Caveat: the redesign bundled accessibility with a broader UX overhaul (accessibility not fully isolated) and is somewhat dated. Cite as a named case study, not a generalizable average.*

**Accenture — *Getting to Equal: The Disability Inclusion Advantage* (2018).** Companies leading on disability inclusion averaged **28% higher revenue, double the net income, and 30% higher economic profit margins** than peers. *Credibility: MEDIUM* — named analyst research, but it measures corporate disability-inclusion practices broadly (workforce + marketplace), not website accessibility specifically, and is a correlation among large enterprises. **Does not scale down to an SMB website** — use as macro context only.

### DIRECTIONALLY USEFUL BUT NOT CITABLE AS AUTHORITATIVE

**Click-Away Pound Survey (2016, 2019) — UK.** 69% of disabled online shoppers will "click away" from a site they find difficult to use; 86% would pay more to buy from an accessible site; 83% limit shopping to sites they know are accessible; estimated UK "lost business" grew from £11.75bn (2016) to **£17.1bn (2019).** *Credibility: MEDIUM for the behavioral percentages* (a genuine, repeated survey of disabled consumers by Freeney Williams/Rick Williams, backed by the Business Disability Forum and RNIB); *LOW for the £17.1bn aggregate* (an extrapolated national figure that does not scale down to a single SMB). Cite the behavioral percentages (69%, 86%, 83%) *with attribution and year*; treat the £-billions figure as context only.

**Tesco (frequently cited).** A reported £35,000 accessible-site investment yielded a large revenue multiple. *Credibility: LOW-MEDIUM* — widely repeated but primary documentation is old and hard to verify; the "37,000% ROI" framing is a roundup artifact. Illustrative anecdote only.

**"$6.9 billion annual loss" for US e-commerce; "$1 → $100 ROI" (attributed to Forrester); vendor case studies (e.g., "ClearVision Optics +19% revenue"); "200–500% first-year ROI."** *Credibility: LOW.* These originate from firms selling audits/CRO (category (a)), or are roundup staples with no locatable primary report, or are absolute aggregates that don't scale. **Exclude or mark low-confidence.**

## 1.2 Hospitality / Hotel-Sector-Specific

**Columbia Human Rights Law Review — *Disabling Travel: Quantifying the Harm of Inaccessible Hotels* (2024).** Academic/legal analysis documenting how hotel websites and reservation systems routinely fail to provide accessibility information and to honor accessible-room bookings, with documented individual financial harms (e.g., a traveler forced to split a vacation across two hotels at ~$700 additional cost). *Credibility: HIGH as a named academic source* for the qualitative/legal argument that inaccessible booking systems drive abandonment and harm. Does not provide a scalable conversion percentage.

**Open Doors Organization / MMGY Global (travel-with-disability research).** ~50% of US adults with disabilities stayed in hotel accommodation in the past two years; MMGY research found **96% of travelers with mobility-related disabilities faced accessibility issues** at hotels/accommodations. *Credibility: MEDIUM* — established travel-research organizations, but figures are usually relayed via vendor blogs; verify against the primary MMGY/Open Doors report before citing. Their travel-spend totals ("$58.2bn," "$13bn," "$19bn") are **market-size figures excluded by the task.**

**"People with disabilities are twice as likely to book a hotel via website than by phone."** *Credibility: LOW/UNVERIFIED* (appears in vendor blogs without primary sourcing). Directionally useful, not citable.

**Mapping note (hospitality):** Hotel-specific *quantified conversion* statistics tied to accessibility are scarce. The honest position: use *cross-sector* multipliers (Deloitte **travel** vertical: +10.1% conversion per 0.1s; Baymard checkout uplift) as the scalable business case, and use Columbia HRLR + MMGY as qualitative evidence that hotel booking engines are a documented, high-frequency failure point.

## 1.3 Revenue-Band / Sector Mapping Model (Accessibility)

- **Scales down to an SMB (usable in a per-client estimate):** Deloitte speed→conversion/AOV multipliers; Baymard abandonment rate and checkout-uplift %; Click-Away Pound behavioral percentages (as "share of a customer segment lost"); the generic model `Annual revenue × conversion-rate delta = revenue gain` (French sources illustrate: on a €500,000-revenue shop, each durable +0.1pt of conversion ≈ several thousand € additional revenue).
- **Does NOT scale down (context only, never a per-client number):** £17.1bn Click-Away Pound; $6.9bn US e-commerce loss; $6T/$13T disability market; all "billions lost" figures; wsc.us.org's $13–18bn (excluded entirely).

# SECTION 2 — SEO

## 2.1 General / Cross-Sector Statistics

### SAFE TO CITE BY NAME

**Pew Research Center — AI Overviews browsing study (published 22 July 2025; data March 2025).** From verbatim Pew: *"Google users who encountered an AI summary clicked on a traditional search result link in 8% of all visits. Those who did not encounter an AI summary clicked on a search result nearly twice as often (15% of visits)."* Only **1%** clicked a link inside the summary; session-end ("zero-click") rose to **26% with a summary vs 16% without.** Methodology: 900 US adults, **68,879 Google searches**, of which **12,593 triggered an AI summary**; **58%** of participants encountered ≥1 AI summary; **18%** of all searches produced one; summaries averaged **67 words** and **88% cited more than three sources.** *Credibility: HIGH* — independent, non-commercial research org, disclosed methodology (KnowledgePanel Digital, ~2.5M page visits). The single best stat for the "SEO is changing, you're losing clicks" argument. **Scales as behavioral percentages.**

**Seer Interactive — AIO CTR studies (2025).** Analyzing **3,119 informational queries across 42 organizations** (25.1M organic + 1.1M paid impressions, June 2024–Sept 2025): organic CTR on queries with an AI Overview fell to **0.61%** vs **1.62%** without; Seer reported a **~61% organic CTR drop and ~68% paid CTR drop** on AIO queries. *Credibility: MEDIUM-HIGH* — reputable agency, disclosed sample/method, but a commercial actor spanning its own client set; corroborated by Pew, so safe to cite with attribution.

**Google/Deloitte *Milliseconds Make Millions*** (see §1.1) doubles as a core SEO/UX-performance stat (Core Web Vitals are a Google ranking signal). *HIGH.*

**Baymard Institute** (see §1.1) is the anchor for checkout/abandonment in the SEO/CRO argument. *HIGH.*

**Schema.org / structured data → CTR (Google-documented named case studies):** **Nestlé — pages shown as rich results had 82% higher CTR** than non-rich pages; **Rotten Tomatoes +25% CTR** on pages with structured data; **Food Network +35% visits** on pages converted to rich-result-eligible; Rakuten users spent 1.5× longer on structured-data pages. *Credibility: MEDIUM-HIGH (Google-documented named case studies).* Broader "rich snippets increase CTR 20–30%" claims trace to Backlinko/roundups — *LOW-MEDIUM; cite as "up to ~30%" directional only.* **Scales as CTR multipliers.**

### MEDIUM / CORROBORATING

**Ahrefs (2025):** ~58% drop in CTR for top-ranking pages when an AI Overview appears (large-keyword analysis); Ahrefs also reports traditional Google organic still drives roughly ~345× more traffic than all AI engines combined (late 2025) — useful for balance. *Credibility: MEDIUM* (reputable SEO-tool company, large datasets, but commercial). **GrowthSRC (2025):** 200,000+ keywords — position-1 organic CTR fell 28%→19% (−32%), position-2 20.8%→12.6% (−39%), 2024→2025. *Credibility: MEDIUM/LOW; corroboration only.*

### NOT CITABLE
- "60% of Google searches are now zero-click" (SparkToro/Datos, 2024) — *MEDIUM; varies by methodology; cite with attribution/year if used.*
- Generic listicle roundups (thedigitalbloom, omnibound, etc.) — *LOW.* Go to the primaries (Pew, Seer, Ahrefs, Google docs) instead.

## 2.2 Hospitality / Hotel-Sector-Specific (SEO)

- **"Near me" search growth:** Google/Skift reported "near me" Google Maps searches grew ~150% YoY (2019 era). *Credibility: MEDIUM but dated;* directional evidence that local/mobile search drives hotel/restaurant discovery.
- **Local SEO / Google Business Profile:** Industry guidance (Mews and others) documents that a complete GBP, consistent NAP, and local content drive map-pack visibility and direct bookings — *qualitative, not quantified; LOW-MEDIUM (vendor guidance).*
- **Google travel-planning behavior:** Google's first-party Think-with-Google travel data documents advance-planning patterns (hotels/flights booked ~12 weeks ahead; experiences later). *MEDIUM (Google first-party), not a conversion stat.*
- **Honest gap note:** hotel-specific *quantified* SEO→booking conversion figures from independent sources are thin. Use cross-sector CTR/zero-click data + the Deloitte **travel** vertical (+10.1% conversion per 0.1s speed) as the scalable business case.

## 2.3 Revenue-Band / Sector Mapping Model (SEO)

- **Scales down:** CTR percentages (Pew 8% vs 15%; position-level CTR; rich-result CTR lift), speed→conversion multipliers, abandonment rates — all ratios usable in "traffic × CTR × conversion × AOV" per-client models.
- **Does not scale down:** industry-wide "X billion searches/day," publisher-traffic-loss aggregates — context only.

# SECTION 3 — GEO (Generative Engine Optimization / AI Answer Visibility)

**Overarching credibility flag:** This field was named in late 2023. Almost all *revenue/conversion* claims come from vendor case studies, agency blogs, and mutually-citing roundups. **There is exactly one peer-reviewed academic anchor (the Princeton GEO paper) and it measures *visibility*, not revenue.** Odysseus should present GEO as "emerging/preliminary — directionally important, not yet backed by citable revenue statistics."

## 3.1 General / Cross-Sector Statistics

### WELL-ESTABLISHED (SAFE TO CITE, with correct framing)

**Princeton (Aggarwal et al.) — *GEO: Generative Engine Optimization*, ACM KDD 2024.** The founding academic paper. Using GEO-bench (~10,000 queries across 9 domains), it demonstrated that targeted content optimizations can **boost visibility in generative-engine responses by up to 40%**, with "Statistics Addition," "Cite Sources," and "Quotation Addition" among the highest-impact tactics; fluency improvements alone produced ~28% gains. *Credibility: HIGH as peer-reviewed research — BUT it measures citation/visibility within a controlled benchmark, NOT traffic or revenue.* Independent critiques note the zero-sum 5-source setup amplifies relative gains and all optimizations were LLM-generated, so real-world effects are likely smaller. **This is your one citable GEO source — cite it as "visibility, up to 40%," never as a revenue figure.**

**Pew Research (see §2.1)** doubles as GEO context: AI summaries cite 3+ sources 88% of the time; most-cited domains are Wikipedia, YouTube, Reddit; .gov appears more in AI summaries (6%) than standard results (2%). *HIGH.* Useful for the "citability" argument (be the source AI quotes).

### EMERGING / PRELIMINARY (cite only as "early data suggests," name the source, flag limits)

**Seer Interactive — ChatGPT conversion case study (Oct 2024–Apr 2025).** For a **single B2B software client**, ChatGPT referral traffic converted at **15.9% vs 1.76% for Google organic** (~9×); Perplexity 10.5%, Claude 5.0%, Gemini 3.0%; ChatGPT visitors viewed ~2.3 pages/session vs 1.2 organic. *Credibility: LOW-MEDIUM — a single-client B2B case (1,370 AI conversions vs ~14M organic sessions); Seer itself cautions the absolute AI volume is tiny.* **Do NOT present 15.9% as a general or B2C/hospitality benchmark.** Honest framing: "AI-referred visitors arrive pre-qualified and, in early studies, convert at higher rates — but from very low volume."

**Visibility Labs / Search Engine Land (full-year 2025, 94 e-commerce sites).** ChatGPT traffic converted at **1.81% vs 1.39% for non-branded organic (+31%)**; ChatGPT sessions grew ~1,079% YoY. *Credibility: LOW-MEDIUM* but more B2C-relevant than Seer. **Conflicting large-scale finding to flag:** a 12-month study of 973 websites (~$20B revenue, Schulze & Kaiser) found ChatGPT referrals *underperformed* Google organic on transactional purchases by ~13% — the reconciliation is that AI over-performs in research-heavy categories and under-performs in impulse categories (apparel, grocery). **State this conflict explicitly in any Odysseus report.**

**Adobe Digital Insights (Q1 2026).** AI-referred retail traffic converted **42% better than non-AI traffic in March 2026** — a reversal from 38% *worse* a year earlier; AI traffic to US travel sites grew ~3,500% YoY (July 2025); ~29–39% of US consumers have used gen-AI for trip planning. *Credibility: MEDIUM* (large first-party analytics: 8M+ travel-site visits, 5,000-person survey), but recent and fast-moving. Present as "early first-party data."

**AI referral share:** ChatGPT drives the large majority of AI referral traffic (Conductor ~87%; eMarketer/Grips ~92%); AI referrals are still ~1% of total traffic (Conductor). *Credibility: MEDIUM (multiple corroborating analytics firms).*

**Structured data → AI citation (VERY PRELIMINARY & CONFLICTING):** Claims include "pages with schema cited 2.3× more in AI Overviews" (Digital Applied), "~40% more citations with structured data" (attributed to UC Berkeley), "71% of ChatGPT-cited pages use schema" — **but Ahrefs found no schema lift across AI sources.** *Credibility: LOW and CONFLICTING.* Do not cite any specific schema→AI-citation number as authoritative; state that structured data is *best practice* for machine-readability and that evidence of its AI-citation effect is preliminary and contested.

**llms.txt:** There is **no credible independent evidence** that an llms.txt file affects AI visibility or traffic; major AI crawlers have not confirmed using it. Present as "proposed/experimental convention, no measured impact."

## 3.2 Hospitality / Hotel-Sector-Specific (GEO)

- **Phocuswright — *The AI Surge* (2026):** **56% of travelers used AI for planning, booking, or in-destination assistance in the past 12 months, up from 43% (H2 2025) and 33% (H1 2025).** *Credibility: MEDIUM-HIGH* (leading travel-research firm, disclosed trend data). Best hospitality GEO stat available.
- **Phocuswright — *Search Slips, AI Surges*:** ~40% of US travelers used gen-AI to plan trips in 2025, an 11-point YoY jump. *MEDIUM-HIGH.*
- **McKinsey (Sept 2025, via Statista):** share of US travelers who have "extensively used" ChatGPT-like tools for trip planning. *MEDIUM (paywalled; cite via Statista/McKinsey).*
- **Industry moves (factual context):** IHG and Wyndham launched ChatGPT apps; Marriott debuted "Ask Bonvoy"; Hilton is piloting an AI concierge; OpenAI's ChatGPT app integrations piloted with Booking.com and Expedia (late 2025). *Credibility: MEDIUM (trade press — Hotel Dive/Statista).* Shows AI booking discovery is real, not hypothetical.
- **Adobe:** ~53% of AI trip-planners use it to find local attractions, restaurants, and "hidden gems" — directly relevant to restaurants/hotels being *citable* in AI answers.
- **Honest gap note:** There are **no** credible statistics linking hotel/restaurant GEO tactics to measurable bookings or revenue yet. State this plainly.

## 3.3 Revenue-Band / Sector Mapping Model (GEO)

- **Scales down (as *ratios*, heavily caveated):** AI-referral conversion multipliers (Seer, Visibility Labs, Adobe) and Princeton's +40% visibility — only as "emerging, direction-of-travel" inputs, not hard forecasts. Traveler-AI-adoption percentages (Phocuswright 56%) scale as "share of your funnel now touching AI."
- **Does not scale / exclude:** absolute "AI will drive $X" market projections; single-client conversion rates presented as benchmarks; contested schema→citation numbers.

# EAA / FRENCH LEGAL FACTS (state as fact, secondary to the commercial argument)

Present these as fact, never as a conclusion, and keep them subordinate to the commercial-loss case in outreach:
- The **European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882)** applies from **28 June 2025** to consumer-facing digital services including e-commerce.
- **Micro-enterprise exemption:** services provided by enterprises with **<10 employees AND <€2M annual turnover or balance-sheet total** are exempt (Article 4(5)/3(23)). Both conditions must be met; the exemption is for *services*, not products.
- **Technical standard:** France's **RGAA** and the European standard **EN 301 549**, both referencing **WCAG 2.1 AA**.
- **French enforcement & penalties (Code de la consommation, art. R451-4; Ordonnance n° 2023-859 of 6 Sept 2023):** the enforcing authority is the **DGCCRF**. Fine for a legal entity: **€7,500 per infraction (cumulative per infraction observed), rising to €15,000 for a repeat offense** (€1,500 / €3,000 for individuals). The DGCCRF can issue an injunction backed by an **astreinte of up to €3,000/day, capped at €300,000.** First French enforcement notices were reportedly issued in November 2025 to major retailers (Auchan, Carrefour, E.Leclerc, Picard).
- **Correct the record internally:** the frequently-miscited "€50,000 + €25,000 / up to €75,000 per service" figures belong to a **separate regime (Loi n° 2005-102, enforced by ARCOM) that targets public-sector bodies and very large private firms (>€250M turnover)** — not ordinary private e-commerce SMBs. Vendor-blog figures of "up to €250,000/€375,000" are **not verifiable against any French primary source and should not be used.**

# RECOMMENDATIONS (for Odysseus report construction)

**Stage 1 — Build the report's spine on HIGH-confidence, scalable stats.** Lead every business case with: (1) Deloitte/Google *Milliseconds Make Millions* (2020) speed→conversion multipliers — use the **Travel** vertical (+10.1% conversion per 0.1s) for hotels/restaurants and **Retail** (+8.4% conversion / +9.2% AOV) for e-commerce; (2) Baymard (2025) 70.22% abandonment + up to 35.26% checkout uplift; (3) Pew (July 2025) 8%-vs-15% AI-Overview click collapse for SEO urgency. All three carry disclosed methodology and scale cleanly to a prospect's revenue.

**Stage 2 — Add one named case study per domain for credibility.** Accessibility: **Legal & General** (W3C-documented: quotations doubled in 3 months, 100% ROI in 12 months, £200k/yr saved, +50% organic traffic). SEO: **Nestlé / Rotten Tomatoes** rich-results CTR (Google-documented). GEO: **Princeton +40% visibility** (peer-reviewed) + **Phocuswright 56%** traveler AI adoption. Always attach (Source, Year).

**Stage 3 — Quarantine GEO as "emerging."** Use a visually distinct "Emerging Research" label for all GEO revenue/conversion numbers. Never present Seer's 15.9% as a general benchmark. State explicitly that GEO revenue-impact data is preliminary and, in one large 973-site study, even contradicted.

**Stage 4 — For French prospects, state EAA facts (secondary to commercial argument).** Use the DGCCRF/€7,500-per-infraction facts above; never the miscited "up to €250,000."

**Thresholds that change the approach:**
- If a prospect qualifies as a micro-enterprise (<10 staff AND <€2M), **drop the legal/EAA angle** and lead 100% on commercial loss — they are exempt, so compliance fear is not credible.
- If GEO acquires a peer-reviewed *revenue* study (not just visibility), promote GEO stats from "emerging" to "citable."
- If a prospect is in an impulse-purchase category (fast fashion, grocery), **down-weight the AI-conversion-premium claim** (the 973-site study shows it may not hold there).

**Build a reusable per-client calculator** using only scalable ratios: `Annual revenue × baseline conversion × uplift % = revenue gain` and `sessions × CTR delta = traffic gain`. Populate uplift ranges from Deloitte/Baymard, not from aggregates.

# CAVEATS

- **Recency & volatility:** SEO/GEO figures (AI Overview coverage, referral shares, conversion premiums) are shifting month-to-month in 2025–2026; date every citation and re-verify quarterly. Seer data shows both a CTR "collapse" (to 0.61% in Sept 2025) and a partial "rebound" (reported ~2.4% by Feb 2026) — the field is unstable.
- **Correlation ≠ causation:** Deloitte (speed↔conversion), Ahrefs/Seer (AIO↔CTR), and the accessibility case studies are largely correlational or bundle multiple changes. Present as "associated with," not "causes."
- **Geographic transfer:** Most conversion/abandonment/AI data is US/UK. Click-Away Pound is UK; Baymard is a global aggregate; Pew/Seer/Adobe are US. French behavior may differ; the French RGAA/EAA regulatory frame is solid, but French-specific *conversion-impact* research is scarce (French-language sources largely re-cite Baymard and Click-Away Pound).
- **Excluded by design:** Population/prevalence and market-size figures ("X million disabled people," "$6T/$13T spending power," travel-spend totals) were kept out of the business-impact citations per the task, even where they appear in otherwise-credible sources (W3C, Open Doors).
- **Vendor bias:** accessiBe, AudioEye, UserWay, Level Access, Be My Eyes, TestParty, and similar sell accessibility products; their case studies may be real but are self-interested — cite only their *named, checkable* case studies, never their aggregate claims.
- **The WSC ($13–18bn) figure and any statistic traceable only to it are excluded entirely.**
