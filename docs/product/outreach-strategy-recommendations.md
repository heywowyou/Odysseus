# Outreach strategy recommendations and six-month forecast

**Written:** 2026-09-13

## Purpose

This document records the pre-launch commercial strategy and six-month sales forecast for the Odysseus outbound channel so future campaign results can be compared against the original assumptions.

The central recommendation is simple:

> Optimize Odysseus for sales per qualified prospect contacted, not for maximum email volume.

Odysseus already has strong advantages over generic cold outreach: qualification before scanning, prospect scoring, prospect-specific technical evidence, a full-site scorecard preview, bounded follow-ups, and manual review. The largest remaining gains should come from better selection, stronger trust, and better handling after a prospect replies.

---

## 1. Six-month sales forecast

The forecast treats 3, 6, or 10 emails per day as **new prospects contacted per working day**, with follow-ups additional.

Assume approximately 130 sending weekdays over six months.

Planning conversion assumptions:

| Scenario | Closed-sale rate per contacted prospect |
|---|---:|
| Conservative | 0.2% |
| Standard | 0.5% |
| Optimistic | 1.0% |

These are planning assumptions only. Actual Odysseus campaign data should replace them as soon as enough observations exist.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3/day | 390 | ~1 | ~2 | ~4 |
| 6/day | 780 | ~2 | ~4 | ~8 |

### Revenue equivalent at the current EUR 1,600 HT full-audit price

| New prospects/day | Conservative | Standard | Optimistic |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3/day | ~EUR 1,600 HT | ~EUR 3,200 HT | ~EUR 6,400 HT |
| 6/day | ~EUR 3,200 HT | ~EUR 6,400 HT | ~EUR 12,800 HT |

This excludes re-audits, referrals, inbound, group expansion, agency partnerships, and future price changes.

For planning purposes, use **about 7 full-audit sales in six months at 10 new qualified prospects per working day** as the standard case.

A rough interpretation at that activity level:

- ~3 sales: weak but plausible result
- ~7 sales: standard planning case
- ~13 sales: strong result
- 10-15+ sales without materially increasing volume: strong evidence that targeting, proof, and sales handling are outperforming the initial model

## 2. Highest-priority strategy recommendations

### 2.1 Establish a clean baseline before optimizing copy

The first meaningful campaign should freeze the current email wording and current preview format long enough to establish a baseline.

The most important first question is:

> Does the current prospect ranking predict reply quality and eventual commercial outcome?

Track:

`rank / score -> reply -> positive reply -> substantive conversation -> purchase`

Avoid changing subject lines, body copy, evidence ranking, scoring weights, and follow-up timing at the same time. A clean baseline is more valuable than premature local optimization.

### 2.2 Evolve scoring toward purchase propensity

The long-term goal is not to identify the worst websites. It is to identify businesses with:

1. meaningful technical pain
2. enough commercial value for the problem to matter
3. realistic ability and willingness to buy a EUR 1,600 diagnostic

A useful future model is:

> Pain x Value x Buyability

Possible inputs should be added only when supported by real campaign outcomes.

Examples:

**Pain**
- booking or transactional problems
- accessibility failures
- search or rendering visibility problems
- GEO / AI-assistant visibility problems
- performance defects

**Value**
- importance of direct booking
- hotel size / capacity
- site complexity
- commercial importance of the website
- portfolio potential

**Buyability**
- commercial positioning
- capacity
- decision structure
- signs of investment in the digital property
- later, observed response and purchase history for similar prospects

Do not over-engineer this before the first campaign produces real data.

### 2.3 Build a strong reply-to-sale process

The biggest conversion opportunity after launch may be what happens after a prospect replies.

Recommended response categories:

- interested / wants details
- asks what was found
- price objection
- already works with an agency
- timing objection / not now
- wrong recipient but willing to redirect

A useful commercial flow is:

`positive reply -> concise human response -> short substantive conversation where useful -> clear offer -> purchase`

Keep the commercial offer simple:

- fixed EUR 1,600 HT price
- clear deliverable
- no improvised pricing
- simple purchase path

Record objection categories so improvements are based on evidence rather than anecdotes.

### 2.4 Turn early customers into proof

The first successful customers are strategically worth more than their invoice value because they can reduce the trust problem for every future prospect.

With customer permission, collect:

- meaningful findings before remediation
- what the client corrected
- what a re-audit verified
- measurable technical improvements where supportable
- a short testimonial
- referral willingness

Avoid unsupported ROI claims.

The first strong case study should be treated as a major commercial milestone.

### 2.5 Consider portfolio potential

Some hotels may belong to groups, collections, management companies, or legal entities controlling multiple properties.

After the baseline experiment, consider identifying whether one successful account could expand to additional eligible domains.

The first contact should remain specific to the scanned property. Portfolio potential matters later in the relationship and may eventually become a ranking input.

### 2.6 Optimize the preview after outcome data exists

The email earns attention; the prospect-specific preview does much of the selling.

Keep current preview evidence selection stable during the baseline.

Later, measure whether certain proof categories correlate more strongly with positive replies and purchases. Commercially salient findings may eventually deserve higher ranking than findings that are technically severe but difficult for a buyer to understand.

Desired feedback loop:

`scanner -> evidence -> preview -> outreach -> commercial outcome -> better evidence ranking`

### 2.7 Test the agency / auditor channel in parallel

Run a small number of exploratory conversations with accessibility specialists, auditors, or web agencies.

Do not build a reseller product first. Ask:

- how they perform automated discovery today
- what they trust or distrust in automated tooling
- what output would save meaningful manual time
- how they would want to use or buy the product

A partner with recurring client demand may have much higher lifetime value than a single direct sale.

## 3. Sending ramp recommendation

Once the sending setup is genuinely ready, use a learning-oriented progression rather than jumping directly to maximum volume:

- first 10 prospects: about 3 new prospects / working day
- next 20: about 5-6 / working day if results are clean
- next 30: about 8 / working day if results remain clean
- thereafter: work toward about 10 / working day while respecting total daily volume including follow-ups

Do not accelerate purely to reach a calendar quota.

At 10 new prospects per day, accumulated follow-ups can already consume much of the intended daily mailbox volume. Better conversion is therefore more valuable than more volume.

## 4. Metrics to track

Core funnel:

`first touches -> all replies -> positive replies -> substantive conversations -> purchase intent -> won -> lost -> re-audit -> referral`

Also preserve where practical:

- score version
- rank / cohort position
- proof-card categories
- campaign version
- objection category
- bounce and suppression outcomes

Initial planning bands:

| Metric | Warning | Healthy | Excellent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Hard bounce | >=2% | <2% | <1% |
| Any reply | <3% | 5-8% | 10%+ |
| Positive reply | <0.5% | 1-2% | 3%+ |
| Sale / contacted prospect | <0.2% | ~0.5% | ~1%+ |

These are directional benchmarks only. Odysseus's own observed funnel should become the source of truth.

Raw reply rate is not enough. A high reply rate dominated by negative responses is not a healthy campaign.

## 5. Review checkpoints

### First 10

Purpose: operational and qualitative validation.

Review:

- deliverability problems
- evidence quality
- weak or confusing proof
- unexpected objections

Do not infer conversion rates from a sample of 10.

### First 100

Purpose: establish the first useful baseline.

Questions:

1. Does score/rank predict reply quality?
2. Which proof categories appear in positive outcomes?
3. Where do positive replies fail to become sales?
4. Are objections mostly about trust, relevance, price, timing, or product scope?
5. Should the scoring model be kept, tightened, revised, or replaced?

### Three months

Review:

- sale rate by prospect segment
- reply-to-sale conversion
- case-study impact if available
- re-audit pipeline
- group / portfolio opportunities
- agency-channel evidence

## 6. Long-term commercial flywheel

The desired progression is:

`better prospect selection`

-> `better replies`

-> `first customers`

-> `case studies and proof`

-> `higher trust`

-> `higher conversion at the same volume`

-> `outcome-trained prospect scoring`

-> `re-audits`

-> `referrals`

-> `portfolio expansion`

-> `agency partnerships`

-> `inbound`

The strongest long-term moat is not the email copy. It is the combination of technical scanning capability, disciplined prospect selection, prospect-specific evidence, and proprietary data showing which businesses and findings actually convert into customers.

---

## 7. Practices not recommended as default growth levers

- multiplying inboxes just to increase volume
- automated high-volume sequencing
- generic AI-written personalization
- changing several campaign variables before establishing a baseline
- optimizing around open rate
- reducing price reflexively because cold prospects do not buy
- unsupported revenue-loss or ROI claims
- adding more follow-ups without evidence that they improve outcomes
